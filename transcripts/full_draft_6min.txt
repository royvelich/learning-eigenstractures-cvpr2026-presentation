Learning eigenstructures of unstructured data manifolds.


[SLIDE 2 · The Laplacian operator — 25 s]
The Laplace–Beltrami operator is one of the most reused objects in
geometry and machine learning. Its eigenbasis powers shape
descriptors, correspondence, manifold learning, mesh smoothing,
geodesics — basically anything that needs a notion of frequency on a
curved domain. This paper is about computing that basis differently.


[SLIDE 3 · applications — 10 s]
Same eigenbasis, six different tools. We want this kind of
versatility — but learnable.


[SLIDE 4 · best k-term basis (curved) — 25 s]
Why is this basis so special? Because on a curved domain, the LBO
eigenbasis is the best k-term basis — the basis that minimises the
reconstruction error of any smooth function, using only k
coefficients. So we want it. The question is: do we have to build
it the classical way?


[SLIDE 5 · DCT special case — 15 s]
The Euclidean version of this story is the DCT. Same picture — best
k-term basis on a flat strip — that's why JPEG works.


[SLIDE 6 · traditional pipeline — 20 s]
The classical recipe is: triangulate the surface, build a cotangent
Laplacian, eigensolve, take the basis. It works — and it is the
bedrock of a thousand papers.


[SLIDE 7 · 3 properties of traditional pipeline — 25 s]
But it has three properties.
[click 1] It's discrete — the operator depends on the mesh.
[click 2] It's explicit — you build the operator before you get the
basis.
[click 3] And it's not differentiable end-to-end. Inside a learning
system, that's a wall.


[SLIDE 8 · Our approach — 18 s]
So we take a different route. We trade operator construction for
probe-function design. We learn a basis whose top-k eigenvectors
minimise the reconstruction error of a chosen family of probes —
implicit, continuous, differentiable.


[SLIDE 9 · Our pipeline — 45 s]
[click 1] Sample a point cloud — a manifold.
[click 2] Sample a family of smooth probe functions on it.
[click 3] A neural network outputs a candidate ordered basis.
[click 4] We project each probe onto the basis and reconstruct it.
[click 5] The loss is the mean reconstruction error across probes.
Minimising it forces the network's output to be the top-k
eigenbasis of the Laplace–Beltrami operator — without ever building
the operator.


[SLIDE 10 · overfit single shape — 30 s]
First check: can it match the gold standard? On a single shape, we
compare our predicted basis to the cotangent Laplacian basis.
[click 3] These are the cosine similarities — green is above 0.75.
The low-frequency basis is essentially perfect, and even at v₂₀ and
v₄₀ we recover the right eigenspaces.


[SLIDE 11 · multi-shape generalisation — 30 s]
Now generalisation.
[click 3] Train on a small collection, test on shapes the network

[SLIDE 12 · unseen shapes — 30 s]
Push it harder.
[click 3] Bull, dragon — completely out of training distribution.
Still recovers the eigenbasis. We are no longer producing a basis
